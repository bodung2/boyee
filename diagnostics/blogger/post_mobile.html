Korea’s 52-Hour Workweek in 2026: The 40+12 Rule Explained

Korea’s 52-hour workweek means 40 regular hours plus up to 12 overtime hours—not a standard 52-hour schedule for every employee.

The short formula is simple, but applying it is not. Breaks are excluded, employer-controlled waiting can count, flexible schedules may shift hours between weeks, and some small workplaces or industries follow different rules. Foreign employees generally receive the same working-time protection as Korean employees.

What does Korea’s 52-hour workweek actually mean?

Under Articles 50 and 53 of Korea’s Labor Standards Act, statutory working time is no more than 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week, excluding recess periods. The parties may agree to as many as 12 additional hours of overtime per week. That produces the familiar maximum of 52 hours.

Part of the weekGeneral limitWhat it means
Statutory hours40 hoursThe normal legal ceiling, excluding qualifying breaks
Extended workUp to 12 hoursRequires agreement; it is not automatically owed by the worker
Combined totalUsually 52 hoursThe general weekly ceiling, not a target schedule

A “week” is a continuous seven-day period, including holidays. Moving work to Saturday or Sunday therefore does not start a separate overtime bucket. The Ministry of Employment and Labor explains that the 2018 reform reduced the former maximum from 68 hours to 52 by treating holidays as part of the same week.

Who is covered—and are foreign workers treated differently?

The working-hour provisions generally apply to businesses or workplaces that regularly employ five or more workers. Article 11 sets that threshold, while the Enforcement Decree explains how the regular employee count is calculated. A workplace with four or fewer regular employees is not simply outside every labor rule, but the core 40-hour and overtime framework does not apply in the same way. The exact headcount and legal entity can matter, so workers should not judge coverage only by how many colleagues happen to be on one shift.

Nationality does not create a separate 52-hour rule. Korea’s official Easy Law service states that foreign workers are entitled to the same working hours, breaks, and leave as Korean nationals. Visa status can affect immigration and workplace-change procedures, but it does not generally erase Labor Standards Act protection.

If you are comparing the weekly cap with pay, see the separate guide to Korea overtime pay in 2026. The two questions are connected but not identical: a schedule can violate an hour limit even when wages were paid, and unpaid overtime can exist below the 52-hour ceiling.

What counts as working time?

The practical question is not only when you entered the building, but whether the time was under the employer’s direction and supervision. Article 50 expressly includes waiting time needed for work when the worker remains under that control.

  • Usually counted: required preparation, mandatory meetings or training, and employer-controlled standby time when you cannot use the period freely.
  • Usually excluded: a genuine recess period that the worker may use freely.
  • Fact-dependent: after-hours messages, early arrival, business travel, remote login time, and informal “voluntary” work. The label alone does not decide the issue.

Article 54 requires at least 30 minutes of recess during four hours of work and at least one hour during eight hours of work. A break is not automatically valid merely because it appears on a schedule. If a worker must keep serving customers, answer calls, or remain ready for immediate instructions, whether that period was truly free can become the central issue.

Can some weeks legally exceed 40 hours?

Yes. Korea allows several structured working-time systems, but “flexible” does not mean an employer may improvise the schedule without legal requirements.

Flexible work hours

For a unit period of up to two weeks, work rules may distribute hours unevenly while keeping the weekly average within 40 hours; hours allocated under that flexible schedule are capped at 48 in a particular week, before any separately agreed overtime permitted by Article 53. For a unit period of up to three months, a written agreement with the employee representative is required, and hours allocated under the flexible schedule cannot exceed 52 in a particular week or 12 in a particular day, before any separately agreed overtime permitted by Article 53. A longer flexible arrangement of more than three and up to six months has added safeguards, including an 11-hour continuous rest period between working days in the ordinary case.

Selective work hours

With a selective system, eligible workers choose starting and finishing times within a settlement framework established through work rules and a written agreement with the employee representative. The settlement period is generally up to one month, or up to three months for research and development of new products or technologies. The legal test focuses on averages and the agreement, not merely on whether the company calls the schedule “flex time.”

Compensatory leave may replace the additional wage for overtime, night, or holiday work only under a written agreement with the employee representative. It is not the same as a manager casually telling an employee to “take Friday off instead.”

What are the important exceptions?

The 52-hour headline has boundaries. The most useful first check is workplace size; the next is the worker’s industry and role.

  • Special industries: Article 59 permits extended work beyond the ordinary 12-hour overtime limit in specified transport and health-service businesses when there is a written agreement with the employee representative. Covered workers must generally receive at least 11 consecutive hours of rest before the next working day.
  • Agriculture and certain other work: Article 63 excludes specified agricultural, livestock, fishery, and certain surveillance or intermittent work from some working-hour, recess, and holiday provisions. Approval requirements can apply to surveillance or intermittent work.
  • Emergency extension: Article 53 allows additional hours in special circumstances with Ministry approval and worker consent, with a narrow route for urgent cases followed by prompt approval.
  • Pregnancy and youth: pregnant employees may not be assigned overtime. Workers aged 15 to under 18 have a lower base limit of 7 hours a day and 35 hours a week, extendable by agreement only up to 1 hour a day and 5 hours a week.

These are defined legal categories, not a general “busy season” exception. A job title such as manager also does not by itself prove exemption from every working-time protection.

How do overtime pay and the hour cap fit together?

At workplaces where Article 56 applies, extended work is generally paid at least 50% above ordinary wages. Night work between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. and holiday work have their own premium rules. Hours can fall into more than one category, so payroll treatment may require more than multiplying every extra hour by the same number.

Also remember the two separate tests:

  1. Was the work allowed? Check the statutory hour limit, consent, written agreements, and any valid flexible system or exception.
  2. Was the work paid correctly? Check recorded hours, ordinary wages, and applicable overtime, night, or holiday premiums.

For the pay side, the 2026 Korean minimum wage guide explains the hourly floor, while the weekly holiday pay guide covers a different entitlement that is often confused with overtime.

What should a foreign employee check in practice?

  1. Confirm the workplace’s regular headcount. Ask how the company determines whether it regularly employs at least five workers.
  2. Keep your own time record. Save schedules, clock records, required chat messages, meeting notices, and instructions that show when work began and ended.
  3. Separate work from real breaks. Note whether you could leave, turn off work duties, and use the time freely.
  4. Ask which system applies. If weekly hours vary, request the work-rule provision and any written employee-representative agreement for flexible or selective hours.
  5. Compare hours with the wage statement. Do not assume that a flat salary automatically includes every overtime hour.

Workers can contact the Ministry of Employment and Labor counseling center at 1350; the ministry lists weekday counseling hours as 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. A labor office can assess a complaint, but coverage and remedies depend on the specific facts. This article explains the general system and is not individualized legal advice.

My take

In my view, the most useful way to understand the 52-hour rule is to stop treating 52 as Korea’s normal week. A clear 40-hour baseline, honest time records, and genuinely usable breaks matter more in daily life than the slogan itself. Rules also work best when employees can understand the schedule before the work is done, not after payroll becomes a dispute.
